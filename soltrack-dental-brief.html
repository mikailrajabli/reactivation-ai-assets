Thesis

Soltrack runs the whole patient conversation for a clinic. It answers, figures out what the patient needs, books them, confirms, reminds, reschedules, checks in after the visit, asks for reviews, catches complaints, and wins back old patients. It works on phone, website chat, WhatsApp and Instagram, writes every booking back into the clinic's calendar and patient software (PMS), and hands anything clinical to a person. We run it for the clinic. They set nothing up. Dental is the biggest clinic market and the one patients come back to most — and the one most crowded with sellers. "An AI that answers the phone" is now the bare minimum (Weave, Arini and Rondah all sell it) and owners are sick of hearing about it. So we don't open with that. We open with the two problems that cost the most money and have the least competition: no-shows (empty chairs) and online reputation. Winning back old patients is the upsell we add later, not the first thing we say.

The five things that matter

  1. No-shows are the sharpest opener. Every owner knows what an empty chair costs. It's the clearest dollar number in the whole practice. About 15% of appointments get missed (study average 15.2%, midpoint 12.9%), and 15–20% in trade data, at ~$200+ of lost work each time est. A big research review (Cochrane) found automatic reminders cut no-shows by about 30–50%. Soltrack goes further: a reminder text, then, if no reply, an AI phone call to confirm, then reschedule the empty slot right there. That catches the patients a plain reminder misses. We can show the before-and-after show-up rate.
  2. Reputation is the second opener, and barely anyone competes here. 87% of people won't consider a business under 3 stars. Reviews are one of the biggest things that decide where a clinic shows up in local search, so a bad rating directly cuts off new patients. Almost nobody does what Soltrack does: after the visit, we check how the patient feels. If they're unhappy, we flag it straight to the owner so they can fix it privately before it turns into a public 1-star. If they're happy, we make it dead easy for them to leave a review. (We have to build this by the rules — see Risks: the FTC and Google both ban filtering reviews.)
  3. The AI receptionist matters, but it's not what we lead with. It counts (about 1 in 3 new-patient calls never get answered vendor-est), but it's the crowded, worn-out thing every seller pitches. We keep it running in the background as the always-on front desk and let the two sharper problems start the conversation.
  4. Winning back old patients is the upsell. A practice is sitting on 800–2,000 patients who stopped coming and about $240k in cleaning revenue they're not collecting. Bringing one back costs $10–30, versus ~$385 to land a brand-new patient vendor-est. It's the best return of anything we do, but we sell it after the no-show and reputation win, as an add-on, not the opener.
  5. The market is crowded and owners are tired of pitches. The way out is the research-report method plus doing it all for them. Weave is public ($249M in revenue). Arini is YC-backed. Dozens of GoHighLevel "dental snapshot" sellers flood the review-automation space. We win by running it as a done-for-you service, by leading with the no-show and reputation angles, and by opening with a real, person-to-person research report instead of another AI demo.

What holds up, plus what's newer this pass: no-shows are the organic pain owners feel, and it's getting worse. A peer-reviewed research review puts missed dental appointments at an average of 15.2% (midpoint 12.9%), with individual clinics running 10–30%; in a 2025 industry poll (MGMA, a big medical-practice trade group), 27% of practices said their no-show rate got worse over the past year (60% about the same, 13% better). The cost is concrete: each missed appointment is roughly $200–$400 in lost chair time (the paid work that would have been done in that slot), and about 35–40% of no-shows are simply patients who forgot — which is exactly what a friendly reminder fixes. The money side is what owners raise on their own (a live r/Dentistry thread this month, "Insight on Buying Dental Practice," runs 22 comments on practice economics). Cancellation fees are a real, live fight, not one we invented. Typical fees run $50–75 and patients push back hard (one this month was charged $100 even after showing up 20 minutes late and calling ahead); dentists say they need the fee, patients call it unfair. That tension is exactly the opening for a friendly confirm-and-reschedule system with no penalty. Reputation moves patients, and how they read reviews is shifting. 83% of Americans check reviews as the first step in picking a dentist, 84% trust them as much as a personal recommendation, 95% say a negative review makes them reconsider, the trust line sits near 4.7 stars, and 76% won't consider a practice whose newest review is over three months old. New this pass: a 2025 patient-review survey shows people now weigh the content and how recent a review is over the raw star number (the star score's share of the decision fell from about 28% in 2024 to about 17% in 2025), and roughly 59% trust a practice more when it replies to reviews — so replying fast and fixing complaints out in the open is now part of the score, not just the average. The review space really is vendor-flooded, and the engine proved it again: the top "dental reputation" items this pass were marketing-agency spam (a "Dental Practice Consulting AI" video with one view, GitHub repos like "Marketing Agency for Dentists" and "local-seo"), not real owners — that's the crowd we stand apart from with the sentiment check, the fast private fix, and done-for-you delivery, not "a review form." AI fatigue is real, and it's our reason to lead with a report, not a demo: dentists trading notes on AI receptionists are split — one said 24/7 coverage "saved $2k/month in missed appointments," another said "patients hung up on the robot voice — lost 3 new patients in one week," and roughly 40% drop the tool within three months. Owners are tired of pitches and wary of vendors, which is why a genuinely useful research report beats another AI demo.

Winning back old patients, and any deeper checks, are conversations we have after the first win.

Offer the owner an honest report on how their practice is doing, do the interview, give them something genuinely useful, and build the relationship. Then let Soltrack come up gently. Never open with "AI," never open with a demo. This works precisely because owners are sick of pitches.

The hook — the report we run first

The Empty-Chair & Reputation snapshot for {{ClinicName}}, built only from public info (don't secretly record their live system): (1) how well the front desk answers — call the line after hours and write down what happened; (2) no-show cost — estimate the empty-chair cost from their public size and the ~15% benchmark; (3) reputation — their current star average, how recent their reviews are, how many reviews sit with no reply, and whether anything catches an unhappy patient before they post. Honest risks

Review rules — the biggest new risk, and we have to build around it. The reputation part can't be "only send the Google link to happy patients." The FTC Fake Reviews Rule (finalized Aug 2024, in effect Oct 2024) bans hiding reviews and asking only happy people for reviews, with fines up to $53,088 per violation. Google's own rules ban filtering reviews too and can wipe a profile's reviews. The legal way to build it: ask every patient for feedback and never block anyone from posting. Our edge is fixing problems fast in private (reach an unhappy patient quickly, sort it out, let them update their review on their own) plus making it dead easy for happy patients to post. That's timing and follow-up, not steering. Done that way it's legal and still something nobody else runs.
Crowded market and pitch fatigue. Weave (public, $249M), Arini (YC), RevenueWell, Dental Intelligence, and a wave of GoHighLevel "dental snapshot" resellers all crowd the answering and review-automation space, and owners are plainly tired of AI demos. What we do about it: don't fight over "AI answers calls / makes reviews." Lead with the empty-chair math and the fast, legal private fix on bad reviews, run it done-for-you, and open with a research report instead of a demo.
Legal paperwork — non-negotiable. We need a BAA (data-handling agreement) with each practice and every vendor we use (Retell signs BAAs). Confirmation and win-back texts need TCPA consent and 10DLC registration (healthcare gets extra scrutiny), so we only text the practice's own patients who opted in. Recording a call needs both-party consent in about 15 states, and AI has to be disclosed (California AB 2905, $500 per undisclosed call), so we build that disclosure into the opening line.
Risk of looking like a scam, and a high bar to earn trust. Owners don't trust flaky AI bots that stop working, and some AI-receptionist products are thin — one reportedly shipped fake testimonials and AI-generated headshots. Buyers want proof, not demos. Our answer: a system tuned and watched by a person that hands off anything clinical, a real named local example, and a measurable before-and-after show-up rate. But if our delivery or credibility slips, we become the very thing they were worried about.
Careful with the no-show promise. How much no-shows drop depends partly on patients, which we can't fully control. So guarantee the thing we do control (booking back a patient) and show the show-up-rate improvement as a real before-and-after. Promising a hard no-show number to a careful buyer will backfire.
Which numbers to trust. The most solid, independent ones: the ~15% missed-appointment rate (peer-reviewed study, average 15.2%), the Cochrane finding that reminders cut no-shows ~30–50%, and BrightLocal's 87% who won't consider an office under 3 stars. The per-empty-chair dollar figures and the win-back stats are practice-management and vendor estimates, so always pitch with the prospect's OWN numbers from the snapshot.
The single highest-leverage move. Land one independent general dentist as a showcase client and bank two numbers from the pilot: the show-up rate before and after the confirmation engine, and the dollars recovered from their inactive patient list. Both are faster and cheaper to produce than proving call-answering, and the no-show + private-fix combo is what no competitor runs as a done-for-you service.

No-show rate
The share of booked appointments where the patient doesn't show up. Dental runs ~15–20% (peer-reviewed average 15.2%); the best-run practices aim to keep no-shows and cancellations together under 10%.
Production / empty chair
Production is the money a dentist earns while working on a patient. An empty chair (a no-show) is about 45–60 minutes of that money lost, roughly $200+ per missed visit est.
Confirmation escalation
Reminder text → (no reply) AI phone call → reschedule. Reminders on their own cut no-shows ~30–50% (Cochrane); the call catches the rest.
Sentiment gate
The after-visit feedback check: unhappy patient goes to the owner for a private fix, happy patient gets a public review link — built without ever blocking anyone from posting (follows FTC and Google rules).
Service recovery
Reaching an unhappy patient fast to fix the problem before it turns into a public 1-star. This is what the sentiment gate really buys you.
Review gating
The banned trick of asking only happy people for reviews or blocking bad ones. Banned by the FTC Fake Reviews Rule (up to $53,088 per violation) and by Google — the thing our design has to avoid.
Star threshold
87% of people won't consider a business under 3 stars (BrightLocal 2024) — why a bad rating cuts off new patients.
Recall / recare
The routine cleaning appointment, usually every 6 months. The steady, repeating revenue.
Active / dormant patient
The rule of thumb: active = at least one visit in the past 18 months. Everyone else is "dormant" — the win-back pool (800–2,000 per practice).
Case / treatment-plan acceptance
The share of the treatment a dentist proposes that the patient actually agrees to. Where the big lifetime value on implants and cosmetic work is won.
PMS · NexHealth Synchronizer
PMS = the clinic's patient/appointment software (Dentrix, Eaglesoft, Open Dental, Curve, Denticon, tab32). NexHealth is the shared connector that reads and writes appointments across 20–40 of these. The AI has to write bookings into it.
DSO
Dental Support Organization — a company that runs many practices from one central office. About 16% of dentists; the enterprise buyer.
PHI · BAA
PHI = protected health info. BAA = a data-handling agreement, required because the AI touches patient data.
TCPA · 10DLC
The consent law and the business-texting registration that cover confirmation and win-back messages.
