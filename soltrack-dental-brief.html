Thesis

Soltrack is a complete patient-communication and conversion system that owns the whole journey — answer, qualify, book, confirm, remind, reschedule, follow up after the visit, capture reviews and complaints, and reactivate — across phone, website chat, WhatsApp and Instagram, written back into the calendar/PMS, escalating anything clinical to a human. Managed, done-for-you. Dental is the largest, most recurring clinic market — and the most incumbent-saturated. "An AI that answers the phone" is now table stakes (Weave, Arini, Rondah all sell it) and buyers are fatigued. So we don't lead there. We lead with the two wedges that carry the most concrete dollar and the least competition: no-show leakage and online-reputation posture. Reactivation is the back-end expansion, not the headline.

The five things that matter

  1. No-show leakage is the sharpest, most concrete wedge. Every owner can tell you what an empty chair costs — it's the clearest dollar figure in the practice. Missed-appointment rates run ~15% (peer-reviewed mean 15.2%, median 12.9%) and 15–20% in trade data, at ~$200+ lost production each est. A Cochrane review finds automated reminders cut no-shows ~30–50%, and Soltrack's escalation — reminder text → AI confirmation phone call → reschedule the gap on the spot captures the patients reminders alone miss. Provable, measurable before/after.
  2. Reputation posture is the second, least-saturated wedge. 87% of consumers won't consider a business under 3 stars; reviews are one of the top local-search ranking signals, so reputation directly gates new-patient flow. Almost nobody runs Soltrack's move: a post-visit sentiment check that instantly surfaces an unhappy patient to the owner for private service recovery — before it becomes a public 1-star — while making it frictionless for happy patients to post. (Must be built compliantly — see Risks: FTC/Google ban true review-gating.)
  3. The AI receptionist / speed-to-lead is context, not the lead. It matters (roughly 1 in 3 new-patient calls go unanswered vendor-est), but it's the saturated, fatigued layer everyone pitches. We include it as the always-on intake spine and let the two sharper wedges open the conversation.
  4. Reactivation is the back-end upsell. A practice carries 800–2,000 dormant patients and ~$240k of dormant hygiene revenue; recovery costs $10–30/patient vs ~$385 to acquire net-new vendor-est. It's the highest-ROI dollar — but sell it after the no-show + reputation win lands, as expansion, not the opener.
  5. Saturation + pitch fatigue is real; the report-interview method + managed delivery is the escape. Weave is public ($249M rev); Arini is YC-funded; GoHighLevel "dental snapshot" sellers flood the review-automation layer. Win on done-for-you delivery, the no-show/reputation wedges run as a service, and a person-to-person research-report opener instead of another AI demo.
Live demand signal (last30days, this run: YouTube + GitHub + a little Reddit/HN; web & Reddit search were largely unreachable, so this is coaching-content-weighted and thin — read as directional, not a census). The organic dental language treats no-shows and last-minute cancellations as the emotional, day-wrecking money leak: a 2025 practice-coaching video frames the target as "cancellations and no-shows combined under 10%," describes a practice that "dropped no-shows to 5% in 60 days," and — the tell that connects both our wedges — notes that when they fixed it gently, "patient satisfaction and reviews actually went up." The live debate is cancellation fees ("$75 fee for under 48 hours' notice") vs. the fear that a fee "drives patients away" — which is exactly the opening for a non-punitive confirm-and-reschedule system. On reputation, the review layer is crowded with cheap GHL snapshot templates (review-generation forms, 52-week SMS nurtures) — so we differentiate on the sentiment gate + service recovery + done-for-you, not "a review form." Patient-side reputation content is enormous (a "dental office red flags" video at 831k views), confirming patients actively judge practices before booking. Pitch fatigue and vendor distrust are visible, which validates leading with a genuinely useful report rather than a demo.
My read — within dental, chase: independent general dentistry for volume (most appointments, so the biggest absolute no-show leak and the recall/reactivation engine), and implant/ortho where a single no-show is a $3,000–$10,000 hole in the day and reputation is do-or-die on high-ticket cases. Reactivation and any verification depth are expansion conversations after the first win.

Copy

The method: a research-report interview, not a pitch. Offer the owner a genuine market report on their practice, do the interview, deliver something useful, build the relationship — then let Soltrack surface softly. Never lead with "AI," never open with a demo. Pitch fatigue is the reason this works.

Lead magnet — the diagnostic behind the report

The Empty-Chair & Reputation snapshot for {{ClinicName}}, built from public data (don't covertly record their live system): (1) front-desk responsiveness — call the line after hours and log what happened; (2) no-show leakage — estimate the empty-chair cost from their public size and the ~15% benchmark; (3) review posture — current star average, review recency, how many reviews sit unanswered, and whether anything catches an unhappy patient before they post. Show it back with their numbers. CTA "want the findings?" — never "book a call."

Honest risks

Review-gating compliance — the sharpest new risk (must design around it). The reputation wedge cannot be built as "only send the Google link to happy patients." The FTC Fake Reviews Rule (final Aug 2024, effective Oct 2024) bans review suppression and selectively soliciting only positive reviews, with civil penalties up to $53,088/violation; Google's own policy prohibits review-gating and can strip a profile's reviews. Compliant build: ask every patient for feedback and never block anyone's public path; the differentiated value is speed of private service recovery (reach an unhappy patient fast, fix it, let them update organically) plus making it frictionless for satisfied patients to post — timing and recovery, not steering. Positioned that way it's legal and still a wedge no one runs.
Saturation + AI-pitch fatigue. Weave (public, $249M), Arini (YC), RevenueWell, Dental Intelligence, and a wave of GoHighLevel "dental snapshot" resellers crowd the intake + review-automation layers, and owners are visibly tired of AI demos. Mitigation: don't compete on "AI answers calls / generates reviews" — lead with the empty-chair math + compliant service-recovery, delivered done-for-you, and open with a research report instead of a demo.
Regulatory — must-do. BAA required with each practice + every subprocessor (Retell signs BAAs). Confirmation/reactivation texts need TCPA consent + 10DLC (healthcare high-scrutiny) → scope to the practice's own consented patients. Two-party recording consent (~15 states) + AI disclosure (CA AB 2905, $500/undisclosed call) → build disclosure into the opener.
Delivery "grift" risk + a high credibility bar. Owners distrust flaky AI bots that plateau, and the market has thin AI-receptionist products — one reportedly shipped fabricated testimonials + AI-generated headshots. Buyers demand proof over demos. Our model (human-tuned, monitored, escalates) + real named local proof + a measurable before/after show-up rate is the answer — but if delivery or credibility slips, we become the objection.
No-show guarantee caveat. No-show reduction depends partly on patient behavior we don't fully control, so guarantee the thing we do control (a reactivated booking) and present the show-up-rate lift as a measured before/after. Over-promising a hard no-show number to a sober buyer backfires.
Data quality. Cleanest independent anchors: the ~15% missed-appointment rate (peer-reviewed systematic review, mean 15.2%), the Cochrane finding that reminders cut no-shows ~30–50%, and BrightLocal's 87%-won't-consider-sub-3-stars. The per-empty-chair dollar figures and reactivation stats are practice-management/vendor estimates — always pitch with the prospect's OWN numbers from the snapshot.
Highest-leverage move. Land one independent GP lighthouse with two banked numbers from the pilot: a before/after show-up rate from the confirmation engine and a recovered-revenue figure from their dormant list. Both are faster and cheaper to produce than call-capture proof, and the no-show + service-recovery combination is what no incumbent runs as a managed service.

No-show rate
Share of scheduled appointments where the patient doesn't attend. Dental ~15–20% (peer-reviewed mean 15.2%); best-managed practices target no-shows + cancellations combined under 10%.
Production / empty chair
Production = revenue a provider generates in the chair. An empty chair (no-show) is ~45–60 min of lost production, ~$200+ per missed visit est.
Confirmation escalation
Reminder text → (no reply) AI phone call → reschedule. Reminders alone cut no-shows ~30–50% (Cochrane); the call captures the rest.
Sentiment gate
Post-visit feedback check that routes an unhappy patient to private service recovery and a happy one to a public review — built without suppressing anyone's public path (FTC/Google compliant).
Service recovery
Reaching an unhappy patient quickly to resolve the issue before it becomes a public 1-star. The value the sentiment gate actually delivers.
Review gating
The prohibited practice of selectively soliciting only positive reviews / blocking negative ones. Banned by the FTC Fake Reviews Rule (up to $53,088/violation) and Google policy — the thing our design must avoid.
Star threshold
87% of consumers won't consider a business under 3 stars (BrightLocal 2024) — why reputation gates new-patient flow.
Recall / recare
Routine hygiene re-appointment (usually every 6 months). The recurring-revenue engine.
Active / dormant patient
Convention: active = ≥1 visit in the past 18 months. Everyone else is "dormant" — the reactivation pool (800–2,000 per practice).
Case / treatment-plan acceptance
The % of proposed treatment a patient agrees to. Where implant/cosmetic LTV is won.
PMS · NexHealth Synchronizer
Practice Management System (Dentrix, Eaglesoft, Open Dental, Curve, Denticon, tab32) · the universal layer that reads/writes appointments across 20–40 PMS. The AI must write bookings into it.
DSO
Dental Support Organization — a group managing many practices centrally. ~16% of dentists; the enterprise buy.
PHI · BAA
Protected Health Info · Business Associate Agreement — required because the AI handles patient data.
TCPA · 10DLC
Consent law + A2P texting registration governing confirmation/reactivation messages.
