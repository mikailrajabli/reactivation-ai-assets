Thesis

Soltrack is one AI that runs a med spa's whole front desk. It works on phone, WhatsApp, Instagram, and website chat. It takes questions, books the appointment, confirms it and chases no-shows, follows up after the visit and asks for a review (with a review sentiment gate — it reads whether the client is happy), and quietly wins back old clients. Everything gets written back into Boulevard/Vagaro (the spa's booking software), and anything medical gets handed to a real provider. Here's the catch: "AI receptionist" is the most over-pitched thing on the internet in this world, so we don't open with it. We open with the two leaks a busy owner feels most and doesn't expect a stranger to name. (1) No-shows and last-minute cancellations — a missed injectable chair is a real $500+ gone. Every spa already sends reminder texts (it's built into Boulevard/Vagaro), and owners say straight out those reminders are weak. We don't sell reminders, we sell what happens after one gets ignored: the AI actually calls to confirm, reschedules on the spot, puts a deposit / card-on-file behind the booking (the one fix owners actually endorse), and backfills the freed chair from the waitlist. That's the easy-to-prove money back. (2) How the spa looks online — >90% of these clients read reviews before they book, so a gate that sends happy clients to Google and quietly catches unhappy ones before they post means real bookings. Winning back old clients and the always-on receptionist are the upsell, not the opening line. Med spas are the way in: +18%/yr, $1.4M average location, 81% single-location independents. No funded rival sells the full done-for-you system, and the law is on our side: booking, intake, and reviews sit on the non-medical MSO side of the wall (the business side, not the doctor side).

The five things that matter

  1. No-shows and cancellations are the clear way in. 12–22% no-show, each empty chair ~$500+, ~$230k/yr at a 15% rate on AmSpa's $527/visit. Every spa already sends reminder texts (built into their booking software) and owners openly call them weak — the fix owners endorse is money down. So Module 2 is the layer after the reminder fails: text ignored → a live AI phone call to confirm, reschedule on the spot, enforce a deposit / card-on-file, and backfill the freed chair from the waitlist. AmSpa/agency data shows this kind of multi-step confirming cuts no-shows from 15–20% to 5–8%. Boulevard's own demo says a 71% average no-show decrease vendor — the best case to point at.
  2. How the spa looks online is the wedge nobody else uses. Med spas live and die by reviews more than any other wellness business — >90% read reviews first and won't book below 4★. Module 3's review sentiment gate (it reads whether the client is happy) sends happy clients to a public Google link and quietly sends unhappy ones to the owner before they post, and tracks the complaint. A peer-reviewed study (JMIR 2024) shows a bad review drops how likely people are to pick that provider 5.05→3.76 on a 7-pt scale, and a reply from the provider pulls it back up 4.07→4.77 — so handling bad reviews directly wins bookings. Almost no competitor sells this.
  3. The open space is the full done-for-you system. Boulevard ($80M), Zenoti ($1.5B), Moxie, Mangomint are software you set up and run yourself; Eva/Clara just answer the phone; Lani is the closest done-for-you option but it serves many industries and has no funding. Nobody funded sells "done-for-you no-show recovery + review sentiment gate + win-back + receptionist, built just for med spas." That's our spot.
  4. Sit on top of their calendar, don't replace it. The big software already holds all their data. The smart move is to plug into Boulevard / Vagaro / Jane / AestheticsPro, not tear them out.
  5. Winning back old clients is the upsell (~45:1), and the market keeps growing. A client is worth ~$9,000 over time vs ~$200 to get a new one; bringing back a client who drifted off costs 5–25× less than finding a new one and pays back 3–8×. Start with no-shows + reviews, then move into their list of old clients. GLP-1 weight-loss meds (72% of clinics) and the Moxie wave of nurse-owned spas keep making new, understaffed buyers.

Fewer legal hoops than dental. Honest risks

Too many pitches / AI fatigue — the biggest risk here. The vendor field is saturated: two passes now confirm a wall of med spa "AI receptionist" products (Clara AI, Appy Pie, Fully Booked AI, NORA by MedSpa Marketing Partners, and YC-backed Tepali) plus the same "voice AI receptionist" GoHighLevel script copy-pasted across every trade — the identical pitch even runs for carpet cleaners. The receptionist itself is being commoditized: Clara AI sells it at ~$497/mo against a ~$4,000/mo human hire, so nobody wins by leading with "we answer your phone." Buyer data backs the fatigue: AI cold email now averages a 3.43% reply rate (about one real conversation per 157 contacts) and the top complaint is that "prospects detect GPT tone instantly" (Prospeo, 2026). So an "AI receptionist" cold pitch sinks instantly. One honest caveat, unchanged after a second search: we did not find med spa owners directly venting about these pitches in Reddit across either pass, so the in-vertical fatigue stays inferred from vendor saturation and cross-industry buyer data, not a med-spa quote. The fix is the whole plan: lead with a genuinely useful report, person to person, never say "AI," never pitch in the outreach — earn the interview, then reveal. If we run this as mass cold-pitching, we lose.
Review-gating rules (the sharp edge of the sentiment gate). Google's rules ban "review gating" — only asking happy customers for public reviews while holding back the unhappy ones. Our gate has to stay within the rules: ask everyone how it went, make it easy for happy clients to reach the public link, and give unhappy clients a private way to sort it out — but never fully block an unhappy client from posting publicly. Framed as "catch complaints privately and respond fast," it's normal reputation management; framed as "only happy people get the link," it risks Google penalties and the owner's trust. Check the exact flow against Google/Yelp's current rules at each launch.
Big software bundling it in. Boulevard (fresh $80M for AI) and Zenoti (built-in AI receptionist) can throw AI reception into a plan their multi-location customers already pay for. The fix: own the small single-location spas they barely serve, sit on top of their calendar, and win on the no-show calling + review sentiment gate + win-back they don't do.
Legal guardrails (steps to follow, not dealbreakers). Never let the AI do or imply the medical exam or give medical advice (CA AB 489 bans acting like you hold a license). Split TCPA consent: reminders = plain consent, win-back/promos = written consent; register 10DLC (healthcare gets extra scrutiny). Tell people it's AI and recorded on every call. Keep HIPAA-level data habits even when cash-pay slips outside "covered entity."
The "scam" worry. Because the market is flooded, owners have been burned — vendors faking results, fake testimonials, flaky bots. They want to see it working live before they trust it. Done-for-you + watched over + sits-on-your-calendar answers the "will it break" fear, but the report must be real and the trial numbers must show up in their dashboard, or we become one more scam they warn each other about.
"Reminders don't work" pushback (from the signal). Owners say straight out that reminder texts are weak and the real no-show fix is deposits/"skin in the game." Sell Module 2 as more than reminders — steps that end in a live AI call and rebook on the spot, and can take a deposit — or the owner files it next to the reminder tools that already failed them.

Sentiment gate
After the visit, the AI checks if the client is happy. Happy clients get pointed to a public Google review; unhappy ones get sent privately to the owner/manager (and tracked), so the problem gets fixed before it turns into a public 1★. Must stay within the rules (see Review gating).
Review gating (risk term)
A Google-banned trick: only asking happy customers for public reviews and hiding the unhappy ones. Our gate has to ask everyone how it went and never stop an unhappy client from posting publicly — sort it out privately, don't silence them.
Reputation posture
How a spa looks online — its star rating, number of reviews, how fast new ones come in, and how it handles bad ones — and how much that drives new bookings. >90% of aesthetic clients read reviews before booking.
No-show call escalation
Module 2 is the layer after the spa's existing reminder is ignored: a live AI phone call to confirm, reschedule on the spot, take a deposit / card-on-file, and backfill the freed chair. Spas already send reminders and owners call them weak, so we never sell reminders — we sell the escalation.
Injectables
Botox and fillers — 45–55% of med-spa revenue.
Good-faith exam (GFE)
The medical check a provider must do before any injectable or laser. A medical act (doctor/nurse practitioner/PA only) the AI must never do or pretend to do.
Medical director
The licensed doctor a med spa runs under. 74% aren't specialists in this field.
CPOM · MSO
Corporate Practice of Medicine rule · Management Services Organization. The medical company handles the actual care; the MSO (which non-doctors can own) runs the front desk, scheduling, and marketing — that's where the AI legally sits.
No-show
A booked client who doesn't show up. 12–22%; each empty chair ~$500+.
Membership
A monthly plan (~$150/mo) that pushes visits from 1.7→3.0 and makes a client worth ~3.5× more.
GLP-1
Weight-loss meds (semaglutide/tirzepatide). Offered by 72% of clinics — a new flood of urgent leads.
Boulevard / Zenoti / Vagaro / AestheticsPro
The booking and client-record systems spas already use. We plug in on top, we don't replace them.
TCPA · 10DLC
The consent law + the business-texting registration. Reminders = plain consent; win-back/marketing = written consent.
