The five things that matter

  1. The whitespace is our exact offer. Boulevard ($80M), Zenoti ($1.5B), Moxie, Mangomint are self-serve software; Eva/Clara answer the phone but don't run reactivation; Lani is the closest managed model but multi-vertical and unfunded. Nobody funded sells "managed AI front desk plus database reactivation, purpose-built for med spas." That is the position.
  2. Ride the calendar, don't replace it. Incumbents own the system of record (booking + EMR). The winning pattern (already how Clara operates) is to plug on top of Boulevard / Vagaro / Jane / AestheticsPro, not rip them out.
  3. The leak is enormous and multi-part. 20–35% of calls missed, 12–22% no-shows (~$500+ per empty chair), 34–40% of bookings after hours skewing to the highest spenders vendor-est. Three recoverable dollars per spa: missed calls, no-shows, and dormant clients.
  4. Reactivation has ~45:1 economics. A client LTV ~$9,000 against a ~$200 CAC; reactivating a lapsed client costs 5–25× less than acquiring one and returns 3–8×. Most spas sit on lapsed lists nobody works.
  5. GLP-1 + the Moxie wave = a rising tide of new, understaffed spas. 72% of aesthetic clinics now offer weight-loss meds (a new high-urgency inbound stream), and Moxie is minting thousands of nurse-founded spas that all need reception + reactivation on day one.
Live demand signal (last30days, r/MedSpa + YouTube, ~last 18 mo). The pain is real and specific — solo front-desk overwhelm ("I am the only front desk person, alone 80% of the time"), booking-software frustration (e.g. Vagaro won't let membership points stack with discounts), and repeat no-shows ("she's missed about $550 worth of appointments"). But the sharpest finding is the sentiment toward AI receptionists: pitch fatigue. One owner-community regular put it bluntly — "when people post about AI, there's almost no engagement, everyone and their uncle are pitching similar services." Owners want proof it integrates and books into their existing CRM and refuse to beta-test unproven tools ("drop any vendor who can't show it working in a live full demo"). Implication: do not run another AI pitch into a saturated channel — lead with a research report + real named proof + integration into their current stack. This is the strongest possible validation of the report-interview method.
Lighter compliance than dental. A cash-pay, cosmetic-only spa may not even be a HIPAA "covered entity," and the front desk is squarely on the MSO/non-clinical side. Permissibility is HIGH — the only wall is: the AI never performs the good-faith exam or gives clinical advice.

Honest risks

Enterprise bundle risk. Boulevard (fresh $80M for AI) and Zenoti (native AI receptionist) can bundle AI reception into a seat their multi-location customers already pay for. Mitigation: own the independent single-location segment they underserve, ride on top of their calendar, and win on managed reactivation they don't run.
Regulatory guardrails (process, not blockers). Never let the AI perform or imply the good-faith exam or give clinical advice (CA AB 489 bars implying a license). Split TCPA consent: reminders = express consent, reactivation/promo = written consent; register 10DLC (healthcare = high scrutiny). AI + recording disclosure on every call. HIPAA-grade hygiene even where cash-pay escapes "covered entity."
Delivery "grift" risk + pitch-saturated channel. Owner communities are flooded with identical AI-receptionist pitches drawing near-zero engagement, and owners demand a live working demo before they trust a tool. Managed + monitored + rides-on-your-calendar answers the reliability fear, and leading with a research report (not a pitch) beats the noise — but slipping delivery or credibility makes us just another pitch.
Data quality. The AmSpa per-spa revenue ($1.4M) and $527/visit are gold; the no-show/missed-call/reactivation percentages are vendor-published — present as ranges and lead with the prospect's own numbers. The 5-minute rule is MIT/Oldroyd 2007, not Harvard (HBR 2011 is the 42-hour figure) — name it right.
Highest-leverage move. Land one independent injectable-spa lighthouse with a recovered-revenue number from its own lapsed list, integrated on top of its existing Boulevard/Vagaro. That single proof unlocks the whitespace no funded competitor occupies.

Injectables
Botox/neuromodulators + dermal fillers — 45–55% of med-spa revenue.
Good-faith exam (GFE)
The provider exam required before any injectable/laser. A clinical act (MD/NP/PA only) the AI must never perform or imply.
Medical director
The licensed physician a med spa operates under. 74% are non-core-specialists.
CPOM · MSO
Corporate Practice of Medicine doctrine · Management Services Organization. The PC holds clinical care; the MSO (non-physician-ownable) runs front desk/scheduling/marketing — where the AI legally sits.
No-show
Booked client who doesn't arrive. 12–22%; each empty chair ~$500+.
Membership
Monthly plan (~$150/mo) that lifts visit frequency 1.7→3.0 and LTV ~3.5×.
GLP-1
Weight-loss meds (semaglutide/tirzepatide). Offered by 72% of clinics — a new high-urgency inbound stream.
Boulevard / Zenoti / Vagaro / AestheticsPro
The booking/CRM systems of record. We integrate on top, we don't replace.
TCPA · 10DLC
Consent law + A2P texting registration. Reminders = express consent; reactivation/marketing = written consent.
