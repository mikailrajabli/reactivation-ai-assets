Who Already Built This

Every market we could find running a new-build-only, builder-verified portal wired to a developer CRM and monetised on leads or attribution — what they charge, how they got traffic, and which ones actually made money · honesty-flagged

Thesis

The headline finding is negative and it is unambiguous: no pure new-build-only portal has ever become a large standalone business anywhere in the world. Every single one either became a brokerage, became builder software, got absorbed, or stayed small — and there are no counterexamples. The strongest proof is NewHomeSource/BDX, the largest new-construction-only portal on earth, which was owned by 24 US homebuilders including Lennar, PulteGroup, KB Home and Beazer, carried 1,200+ builders' inventory for 24 years, and was sold to a data company in March 2024 at an estimated ~$20.5M of revenue. If a builder-owned new-build portal were a good business, Lennar and Pulte would not have sold it. Canada's BuzzBuzzHome went the same way. Lamudi tried the new-build playbook on three continents and exited all three. And PropertyGuru's FastKey — the exact portal-plus-developer-CRM bundle, with 500+ projects, 100 developers, e-booking, live inventory grids and free access to Southeast Asia's #1 property audience — was shut down in August 2023 with an S$8m impairment. But the scan also found the one shape that works, and it is not a portal at all: Poland's Property Group runs RynekPierwotny.pl (new-build only) plus two owned CRMs plus a broker network plus a data business, and does 70M PLN of revenue at roughly 28% net margin, entirely bootstrapped. The lesson repeats in every region: the portal is the demand harvester, the CRM is the meter, and the money is on the transaction.

The five findings that matter most

  1. The commission pool is 2–8× the entire marketing budget you'd otherwise compete for. A developer's total digital marketing wallet runs 0.8–2.5% of unit price (Brazil CAC data) or 2–4% of revenue for multi-family (Russia). The developer-paid agency commission is 2–8% — Dubai 2–8% spiking to 12% on launches, Egypt 2–5%, Russia 2–5%, Brazil launches 4–6%, India 1–4%, Pakistan's Zameen 10–15% on exclusive mandates. Published portal subscriptions top out around €290–600 per project per month (Otodom) versus roughly €30,000 of commission on a single 5% off-plan unit. Anyone selling into the media budget is fighting Meta and Google for the smaller pot.
  2. The neutral attribution registry does not exist anywhere on earth — and two of the world's largest new-build markets independently invented the same primitive for it. China's 报备 and India's channel-partner registration converged, with no contact between them, on: pre-register the buyer before any visit → approval opens a 7/15/30-day protection window → the broker must accompany the buyer on the first sales-office visit → unvisited claims auto-release → disputes resolve by system timestamp > audit record > visit confirmation > signed docs. That convergence is the strongest design signal in the entire scan. Yet every implementation sits inside one party's system. Profitbase's own industry roundtable names the gap: a shared agent database "remains unbuilt because competitors won't cooperate."
  3. Nobody won by buying traffic. There are exactly three cold-start strategies that worked. Inherit an audience (sahibinden rode Turkey's dominant general classifieds — the cheapest start in the scan; Batdongsan was already #1). Consolidate it (99 Group bought the #2 and #3 portals to become #1; Hepsiemlak absorbed Zingat; DCG rolled up Lamudi + Bproperty; QuintoAndar bought Navent's portals with Tokko CRM). Or own the supply and the registry first (Beike spent 17 years building a brokerage and the Housing Dictionary before opening the network; Property Group became the market's source of statistics). The counter-evidence is brutal: Info Edge spent ₹373 crore on advertising in FY25 and 99acres is still barely cash-positive after ~15 years; NoBroker burned to a ₹411 crore annual loss. Paid acquisition is not a fourth option.
  4. Open transaction data is a revocable government policy choice, never a legal entitlement — and the Dubai story is widely misreported. Dubai Law No. 7 of 2006 actually restricts register access to interested parties, judicial authorities and delegated experts. Publication happens under the 2015 Dubai Data Law, where which datasets are classified "Open" is a discretionary decision of the data provider — not an itemised statutory list. Saudi is the same shape: SREM's per-m² price disclosure was a ministerial launch (Sept 2023) under SDAIA's "open by default" umbrella, not a publication mandate. And Abu Dhabi, in the same federation, chose differently and publishes only aggregates. Separately: many blogs claim the Dubai Land Department owns DXB Interact. It does not — the brokerage fäm Properties built and funds it, with Firas Al Msaddi as CEO of both.
  5. Pure new-build is violently cyclical, so underwrite for a halving. Zameen fell from ~$32M to ~$15M of revenue in 24 months with EBITDA margin collapsing 20% → 4.29%; its own analysts called the development segment "too cyclical" and noted "no amount of data can make you immune from that boom and bust curve." Beike's Q4 2025 GTV fell 36.7% YoY. OLX Brasil's operating margin went 27% → 2% in one year. Lopes' VGV fell 6% in 2025. La Haus cut 54 roles the moment Mexican rates moved; Loft cut over 1,200. The hedge the data supports: the attribution product is counter-cyclical — commissions rise when sales slow, with Russian developer-agency rates climbing from ~1.5% (2023) to ~2.4% (2025) precisely because agencies fight harder over lead ownership in a downturn.
My read. Three things to take from this. First, do not build a business whose success case is developers paying a monthly fee to be listed — that is the most-attempted and least-successful plan in the dataset, and there is a live example (XPLOON, UAE, April 2025) launching it right now with explicitly zero fees from either side and no revenue model. Second, the defensible asset is a canonical unit-level registry plus a neutral attribution ledger, because Beike proves attribution is unenforceable without unique unit identity, and because everyone says the neutral layer is needed and nobody has built it. Third, the honest ceiling: RynekPierwotny is the best outcome in the dataset at ~$17M revenue in a country of 38 million, and it took 15 years, four owned assets and zero outside capital to get there.

The money is in the layer Korter never built.

The archetype trap. Archetype C is the intuitive plan — build a nice portal, bundle a CRM, charge developers monthly. It is also the plan with the clearest failure evidence in the dataset, and the failure mechanism is cannibalisation: a CRM that raises the developer's own conversion rate reduces how many leads he needs to buy from you. You cannot monetise both sides of that trade. FastKey had 500+ projects, 100 developers and free access to the region's biggest property audience, and PropertyGuru still killed it because it failed the "scalable growth with strong unit economics" test. Its revenue was never even broken out — the whole segment containing it was ~4% of group revenue while agent subscriptions compounded past it.

Attribution — the unclaimed position

This is the most valuable thing the scan found. The developer↔agency "whose client is this" conflict exists in every market examined, costs real money, and is named explicitly by practitioners. Tooling exists everywhere — but always inside one party's system. No neutral, cross-market registry exists anywhere in the world. And the design for one is already proven twice over.

Beike's ACN — the closest thing to a working neutral registry

Beike decomposes one transaction into 10 separately-credited roles, five on each side:

Listing side (房源方) ≈ 40% of commissionBuyer side (客源方) ≈ 60% of commission
房源录入人 — listing enterer (first to put it in ACN)客源推荐人 — buyer referrer (first to engage the buyer)
房源维护人 — listing maintainer (hosts viewings)客源成交人 — closing agent (~30% of total commission)
房源实勘人 — inspector (survey, photography, VR)客源合作人 — cooperating agent
委托备案人 — mandate/documentation handler首看人 — first-viewing agent (paid anti-poaching credit)
钥匙持有人 — key holder交易/金融顾问 — transaction & finance advisor

Splits are set city by city — 一市一策, "one city, one policy." Not a global constant. Underlying gross resale commission was standardised at 3% total (1% seller + 2% buyer) across Beike-system brands from Feb 2021.

Enforcement — the part everyone misses

Attribution is enforced by a credit score with real teeth, not goodwill:

The 12-point licence

An annual 12-point system, like a driving licence, points reset yearly. 500+ specific rules across ~100 violation categories. Penalty ladder: minor −1, warning −3, yellow line −6, red line −12 (score wiped, must re-qualify), black line = permanent platform ban.

Why it works

The score gates traffic allocation, listing access, and which ACN roles you're permitted to hold. Cheating costs you future income, not a fine. Agents join ACN individually and exclusively, carrying a personal score.

And the referee is a jury of peers, not the platform. Three agent-run bodies participate in rule-making and enforcement: the New Brokerage Brand Alliance, the Store Owner Committee, and the New Brokerage Jury (新经纪陪审团) — a jury of working agents that is the final arbiter for commercial disputes between agents, and between agents and Beike's own departments. This is the single most important governance detail in the scan: a registry that adjudicates its own disputes isn't neutral. A peer jury is what makes rivals accept the referee.

The primitive two giant markets invented independently

China's 报备 (baobei) protocol and India's channel-partner registration converged on the identical mechanism, with no contact between them. That is the strongest design signal in the entire scan.

1
Pre-register the buyer in the system before any site visit, and get approval. In India: timestamped registration inside the developer's CRM recording date, time and source.
2
Approval opens a protection window — 7 / 15 / 30 days, chosen per project by sales velocity.
3
The broker's representative must accompany the client on the first sales-office visit for the claim to count. Both markets require this.
4
Unvisited registrations auto-release at expiry (防撞单, "anti-collision") so nobody can hoard leads.
5
Disputes resolve by a fixed evidence hierarchy: system timestamp > audit record > visit confirmation > signed subscription documents. In India: "if two channel partners claim the same buyer, the timestamp settles it."

The gap, stated by the industry itself. Profitbase's own industry roundtable names the failure directly: agents are confused "не потому, что они не могут диджитализоваться… а потому что у каждого застройщика по-своему" — not because they can't digitalise, but because every developer does it their own way. A proposed «Единая правдивая база агентов» (single truthful agent database) remains unbuilt because competitors won't cooperate. That is a market failure, not a technology gap — and market failures are exactly what a neutral third party is for.

The prerequisite nobody can skip

Attribution is unenforceable without a canonical unit identity. Beike's ACN only functions because the Housing Dictionary (楼盘字典) gave every unit a unique ID: 250M+ property records / ~187M homes across 450,000+ communities and 325+ cities, covering >90% of tier-1/tier-2 housing, ~1,200 data points each — collected on foot by its own agents over 17 years. Every commission dispute reduces to "which unit, which buyer, which timestamp," and none of those is answerable without a registry. The good news for a new-build-only scope: you are indexing a few thousand projects and their unit stacks, not 250 million resale homes. The registry is the moat, not the interface.

Why Beike is not a counterexample to "don't be referee and competitor"

Beike appears to break the rule — it runs Lianjia, the largest brokerage, and the network that referees everyone. Note the deliberate structure that made it survivable: Lianjia is one branded participant inside ACN; rules are co-written by the Store Owner Committee and Brand Alliance; disputes go to the agent jury; and by 2021 ~406,000 active agents were on ACN with only ~25% wearing the Lianjia badge, cross-store cooperation at 75%, and non-Lianjia resale GTV of RMB 928.1bn. The network outgrew the house brokerage before anyone had to trust it. Contrast REA India, which ran a broker (PropTiger) and an ad platform (Housing.com) side by side and sold the broker in July 2025 for ₹86.45 crore — 11% of a ₹711 crore revenue base, all-stock, at a brutally low price.

The playbook

Cold start — the only three strategies that worked, ranked by cost

1
Own the supply and the registry first, then open the network. Beike spent 2001–2018 building a first-party brokerage and the Housing Dictionary before opening ACN — and by 2021 only ~25% of its 406,000 active agents were Lianjia. Property Group became the market's source of statistics (top-3 Google on primary-market queries, 9,700+ expert citations in 2023, a free training academy, a quarterly trade magazine), then monetised the audience. Cheapest in cash, most expensive in time.
2
Go broker-first, not consumer-first. Reelly never fought a consumer SEO war with Bayut or Property Finder. It gave agents a free tier plus the one thing they cannot get anywhere else — live availability, commission rates and payout terms in one place — then sold seats to agencies, then sold developers access to that agent network. Nawy did the same with cash: it paid partner brokers their commission upfront from its own balance sheet, funded by $23M of bank debt alongside $52M equity, and went from 3,000 to 6,500+ brokerage firms in seven months. This is the cheapest viable path for a small team.
3
Inherit or consolidate an existing audience. sahibinden free-rode Turkey's dominant general classifieds. 99 Group bought the #2 and #3 portals to become #1. Hepsiemlak absorbed Zingat. DCG rolled up Lamudi + Bproperty. QuintoAndar bought Navent's portals together with Tokko CRM. Requires capital or an existing asset.
Not an option: buying demand. Info Edge spent ₹373 crore on advertising in FY25 and 99acres is still barely cash-positive after ~15 years. NoBroker burned to a ₹411 crore annual loss. Housing.com grew 25% and remains A$28.4m EBITDA-negative. Nobody in this scan won by buying traffic.

The ten things most worth copying

  1. Build the unit-level registry before the marketplace. Attribution is unenforceable without canonical unit identity — that is why Beike's Housing Dictionary preceded ACN. For new-build-only scope this is a few thousand projects and their unit stacks, not 250 million homes. Never let a project onboard without unique unit IDs.
  2. Ship the 报备 protocol verbatim as the core object, not a feature. Pre-register → 7/15/30-day window → broker-accompanied first visit → auto-release on expiry → timestamp-first dispute hierarchy. Two of the world's largest new-build markets invented it independently.
  3. Split commission by role, not by side, and make the split a per-city config. Beike's 10 roles, ~40/60 listing/buyer, ~30% to the closer, and 一市一策. Include the first-viewing agent role — it is a paid anti-poaching guarantee, and it is what makes agents trust the system.
  4. Enforce with a licence-style credit score; adjudicate with a peer jury. 12 annual points, tiered penalties, and the score gating traffic, listing access and role eligibility so cheating costs future income. Final arbitration by working agents, not your support team — a referee that judges its own disputes isn't neutral.
  5. Make base listing free to win total coverage, then charge for attention and outcomes. LUN states the reason plainly: base placement is free so all new builds are equally accessible. Korter, Krisha and RynekPierwotny (299 zł token floor) all do it. A portal missing 40% of a city's projects is worthless to buyers, and the buyer side is what developers actually pay for.
  6. Solve data freshness with paid editorial ops, and make it the moat. LUN photographs every construction site and checks prices by hand, monthly. Reelly admits half its pipelines are automated and half manually curated daily. DOM.RIA built a brand on inspector-verified listings. Property Group's BIG DATA monitors ~90% of new stock independently of what developers submit. Every durable player concluded developer self-service data decays — and the resulting photo time series is unscrapeable.
  7. Define the billable event precisely, publicly, and in your favour. Cian's entire developer business rests on one sentence: a billable call is 30+ seconds, counted even if no conversation happened, deduped per calendar month. That produced +18.2% growth in a falling market. Note the exploitable spread — developers' own qualification bar is 60+ seconds with agreed budget and timeline.
  8. Price the broker seat publicly and cheaply; keep the developer price bespoke. Reelly is the only product in the scan with a real published sheet, and only on the agent side. Transparent self-serve where you need volume; negotiated enterprise where the budget is.
  9. Make verification require a counter-signature and give it an expiry. Bayut's off-plan TruCheck demands an Oqood or SPA plus a developer NOC and expires after 45 days — badges rot rather than lie. Saudi's Wafi makes it statutory: developer registered, project licensed, design approved, escrow mandated before a single unit can be sold. In a market with no Wafi, you can be the private Wafi.
  10. Consume the state's verification instead of manufacturing your own, where it exists. India's RERA Section 11 legally compels every developer to publish, quarterly, on a public state portal, the up-to-date number and types of apartments booked, garages booked, approvals taken/pending and project status — MahaRERA exposes total inventory with sold and unsold units. Vietnam gates off-plan sales behind a per-buyer bank guarantee and a 5% deposit cap. Both are free "builder-verified" signals a portal can ingest. Caveat: RERA is aggregate counts and PDFs, not a live unit feed, and quality varies sharply by state.
The free-data top-of-funnel play, and its sequel. fäm Properties — a brokerage — built DXB Interact on free Dubai Land Department open data, gave it away with no login, and bought years of organic authority for the cost of a small dev team, out of a DLD collaboration signed in Q4 2021. Then note what happened next: Dubizzle Group bought Property Monitor outright in April 2025 rather than build analytics. The free data layer is the cheapest customer acquisition in real estate — and it is also the thing that gets acquired.

What to avoid

1. Do not sell per-project developer SaaS as a business line. This is the clearest failure in the dataset. FastKey had everything: live unit inventory grids, e-booking with deposit collection and paperwork generation, 360° StoryTeller walkthroughs, channel distribution to agencies, analytics — plus 500+ projects, 100 developers, and free access to Southeast Asia's #1 property audience. PropertyGuru killed it in August 2023 with an S$8m impairment for failing the "scalable growth with strong unit economics" test. The tell: FastKey's revenue was never broken out, and the entire segment containing it was S$1.5m of S$37m quarterly revenue (~4%) while agent subscriptions compounded past it. Per-project SaaS means bespoke onboarding, bespoke inventory modelling, long sales cycles, and churn the moment a project sells out. It does not compound. Give the CRM away as the meter that captures the transaction; charge on the transaction.
2. Do not be both the neutral referee and a competing broker. REA India ran PropTiger (broker) and Housing.com (ad platform) side by side and sold the broker in July 2025 for ₹86.45 crore — 11% of a ₹711 crore revenue base, all-stock, at a punishing multiple — while keeping the platform. A registry that competes for the buyer cannot credibly adjudicate whose buyer it was; brokers will not feed inventory or leads to a rival's referee. Beike is the apparent exception only because the network outgrew the house brokerage before anyone had to trust it, with co-written rules and a peer jury. Reproducing that governance is hard; competing with the people you referee is easy and fatal.
3. Do not escalate into inventory guarantees or financing when developers won't pay for leads. This is La Haus's exact path and it is the most expensive lesson available. New-build-only marketplace on 3–5% developer commission, ~$200M raised including Bezos Expeditions. Developers wouldn't pay enough for leads alone, so in Aug 2022 it launched "La Haus Now" — financing developer inventory to guarantee "almost 100% certainty that you will be moving a minimum amount of inventory in a period of three months," 150 units/month capacity, 19 developers, already 15% of income at launch. Then 54 layoffs (Nov 2022) when Mexican rates moved, then fractional-ownership products, and today ~100 people selling AI tools to builders. Buying the risk is what you do when the model doesn't work; it doesn't make the model work.
4. Do not assume open transaction data will arrive. Dubai Law 7/2006 restricts register access; publication rests on the 2015 Dubai Data Law where "Open" classification is a discretionary decision. Saudi's SREM was a ministerial launch, not a mandate. Abu Dhabi, in the same federation, chose to publish only aggregates. Any plan whose confidence model depends on a national price register opening must be a negotiation, not an entitlement — and must survive the register never opening.
5. Underwrite for a halving, because the segment is violently cyclical. Zameen: ~$32M → ~$15M revenue in 24 months, EBITDA margin 20% → 4.29%, and its own analysts called it "too cyclical… no amount of data can make you immune from that boom and bust curve." Beike Q4 2025 GTV −36.7%. OLX Brasil operating margin 27% → 2% in a year. Lopes VGV −6% in 2025. Loft: >1,200 layoffs. Any model that only works at peak transaction volume is not a model. The available hedge: the attribution product is counter-cyclical, because commissions rise when sales slow.
6. Beware the capped-commission market. Turkey caps total service fee at 4% excl. VAT (2% buyer + 2% seller) under the Taşınmaz Ticareti Yönetmelik. Consequence: no Turkish portal monetises developers on commission — every one of them sells store subscriptions and ads instead. Check the regulatory cap before pricing any commission-share model in a new market.
7. Note the two live warnings in-market right now. XPLOON (UAE, April 2025): developer-only listings, brokers barred, "no commission fee charged from either buyers or real estate developers", eight developers onboarded, no revenue model at all. And the UK's iPropertyCompany (2014) — banned estate agents, developers and private sellers only, free listings, NHBC-registered builders ranked top — with no evidence of continued operation. The purest expression of the idea has been attempted repeatedly, and the recurring missing piece is always the same: who pays.

The honest ceiling

The best outcome in the entire dataset for a new-build-focused business that is not a pure brokerage is Property Group: ~70M PLN (~US$17M) revenue, ~28% net margin, in a country of 38 million — after 15 years, four owned assets, and zero external capital. The best pure-play new-build brokerage, Lopes, nets ~US$38M on R$12.9bn of intermediated volume — 25 years, listed, and a 1.57% net take. Nawy is the fastest riser at $1.4bn GMV and profitable, and it is a full-stack broker with mortgage, fractional ownership and asset management bolted on. Those three are the realistic shapes of success. None of them is "a portal."

Glossary

The vocabulary of this category, across the markets where it actually operates. Using it correctly is the difference between sounding like an operator and sounding like a tourist with software.

报备 (bàobèi) — China
Lead pre-registration. A broker registers a buyer in the developer's system before the first visit; approval opens a 7/15/30-day protection window. Paired with 判客 (pànkè), adjudicating whose client it is, and 防撞单, anti-collision auto-release of expired claims.
ACN — Agent Cooperation Network (China)
Beike's inter-agency commission-split system. Ten separately-credited roles, ~40% listing side / ~60% buyer side, ~30% of total to the closing agent, splits configured per city (一市一策).
楼盘字典 — Housing Dictionary (China)
Lianjia's canonical property registry: 250M+ records, ~187M homes, 450,000+ communities, ~1,200 data points each. The prerequisite that makes ACN enforceable.
贝壳信用分 — Beike credit score
Annual 12-point licence, 500+ rules, penalties −1/−3/−6/−12/permanent ban. Gates traffic, listing access and role eligibility.
Фиксация клиента (fiksatsiya) — CIS
Registering a buyer to a specific agency so commission is owed to them. The source of most developer-agency conflict. Each developer sets its own duration in its own регламент — 3 working days, 7 days and up to 1 month are all in use.
Шахматка (shakhmatka) — CIS
Literally "chessboard": the unit-inventory grid, floor × block × status × price. The core object of any developer CRM. Profitbase sells a web version at 20,700 ₽/mo per site.
Целевой звонок — "targeted call" (Russia)
The billable event in portal CPA. Cian's definition: 30+ seconds, counted even if no actual conversation took place, deduped per calendar month. Developers' own qualification bar is typically 60+ seconds with agreed budget, timeline and location.
Channel partner / CP — India
A RERA-registered broker selling a developer's inventory on commission (1–3%, to 5% luxury). ~70% of Indian residential sales flow through them. Attribution runs on timestamped CRM registration plus a mandatory CP-accompanied first site visit.
RERA Section 11 — India
Statutory quarterly public disclosure: every registered promoter must publish, on the state RERA portal within 15 days of quarter end, the up-to-date number and types of apartments and garages booked, approvals taken and pending, and project status.
Wafi — Saudi Arabia
The statutory off-plan sales licensing regime. Registers the developer, licenses the project, approves the design and mandates escrow before any unit may be sold. There is no equivalent in most markets — which is the opening for a private version.
Oqood / NOC — UAE
Oqood is the DLD off-plan registration record. A developer NOC (no-objection certificate) permits resale. Bayut's off-plan TruCheck requires an Oqood, or an SPA plus NOC, and expires after 45 days.
VGV — Brazil
Valor Geral de Vendas, total sales value intermediated. Lopes reported R$12.9bn of VGV in FY2025 against R$203.1M of net revenue — a 1.57% net take.
GTV / GMV
Gross transaction/merchandise value. Beike FY2025: RMB 3,183.3bn total GTV, RMB 890.9bn new-home. Nawy: $1.4bn GMV in 2024.
Lançamento — Brazil
A new-build launch. Launch commission tables run 4–6%. ZAP's lançamentos product is a free partnership layer that feeds paid agent subscriptions.
Preventa — LatAm
Pre-sale / off-plan. Notably, no dominant preventa specialist exists in Mexico.
Taşınmaz Ticareti Yönetmelik — Turkey
The Regulation on Real Estate Trade (2018, amended May 2025) capping total service fee at 4% excl. VAT, customarily 2% buyer + 2% seller. It forecloses commission-based models in Turkey.
PF Flex — UAE
Property Finder's dynamic credit pricing: 1 credit per listing in 460+ communities (~95%), more credits where a community delivers higher leads-per-listing or faster conversion.
Platforma Mieszkaniowa — Poland
Property Group's developer↔agency layer: de-duplicates and verifies client submissions, free for agents, developer-funded commission paid within 3 days of sale, one contract and one coordinator per developer.
İlkin / təkrar — Azerbaijan
Primary / repeat registration of ownership rights. A registration-sequence concept, not a market-segment concept — do not present it as "primary sales."
